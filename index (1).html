Orbits & Think-Pair-Share

Falling… and missing the ground

An orbit is a projectile moving so fast that the planet's surface curves away as fast as the object falls. Newton's cannon, scaled up to space.

Think · Pair · Share

Prompt: At 400 km altitude gravity is still about 90 % as strong as on the ground. So why do astronauts on the ISS float? And what would happen if the station suddenly stopped moving sideways?

Show sample answers

Think: Astronauts and the station fall together, so nothing pushes on them — weightlessness is free-fall, not zero gravity.

Pair: The sideways speed (~7.7 km/s) makes the ground curve away as fast as they fall, so they keep missing Earth.

Share: With no sideways speed the station would fall almost straight down and re-enter the atmosphere.
